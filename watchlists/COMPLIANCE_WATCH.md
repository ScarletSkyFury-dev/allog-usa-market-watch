# Compliance Watchlist

Track these topics for **fresh developments only**. Do not automatically repeat them in each weekly edition.

## 1. English Language Proficiency (ELP)

### Why it matters
Current enforcement can remove drivers from service, reducing the legally usable driver pool.

### Watch for
- FMCSA / Federal Register rulemaking status
- comment deadlines
- final-rule publication
- new out-of-service statistics
- state enforcement changes
- cross-border / commercial-border-zone effects

### Current editorial framing
Always distinguish the **existing enforcement regime** from any **proposed permanent federal rule**.

Potential client impact: reduced driver availability, particularly in cross-border, drayage, and owner-operator-heavy markets.

---

## 2. ELD removals and deadlines

### Why it matters
A commercially available carrier can become unusable if the driver is operating with a revoked ELD after the transition deadline.

### Watch for
- newly revoked devices
- replacement deadlines
- CVSA/FMCSA enforcement dates
- out-of-service consequences
- large carrier/vendor exposure if documented

Do not repeat old device lists after the deadline unless there is a new enforcement effect.

---

## 3. CDL training / Training Provider Registry enforcement

### Why it matters
Removal of noncompliant training providers and licensing scrutiny can reduce the pipeline of legally qualified drivers.

### Watch for
- number of schools/providers removed
- FMCSA audits
- state CDL-program findings
- driver-license revocations
- new federal investments or enforcement programs

---

## 4. Non-domiciled CDL

### Why it matters
Changes to eligibility and verification can directly affect driver availability, especially in cross-border and owner-operator-heavy segments.

### Watch for
- final federal rules
- state implementation
- mass revocations / re-verification
- court action
- measurable capacity impact

Do not include as background-only filler.

---

## 5. High-risk carrier enforcement / FMCSA modernization

### Why it matters
Carrier investigations, shutdowns, authority controls, and modernization initiatives can shrink or reshape usable capacity.

### Watch for
- high-risk carrier investigations
- voluntary cessations / shutdowns
- FMCSA staffing/funding changes
- new-carrier safety-readiness requirements
- single vetted registration portal proposals
- continuously updated fit/unfit safety ratings
- real-time CDL status alerts
- crash-data / public-reporting reforms

Separate **enforcement results** from **TAEC/TCA advocacy proposals**.

---

## 6. MC-number trafficking / carrier identity risk

### Core issue
MC numbers and carrier authorities are being marketed online as access credentials, not just identifiers. Established authority age, platform approvals, apparent history, and freight-program access can make an authority more valuable to bad actors.

### Preferred source hierarchy
1. FMCSA official guidance / enforcement position
2. AlphaLoops underlying research
3. current independent industry reporting
4. older industry reporting for historical mechanics only

### Watch for
- FMCSA enforcement tied to authority sales
- ownership / legal-name inconsistencies
- new AlphaLoops or similar research
- broker/vetting-platform responses
- carrier identity verification changes
- fraud / cargo theft cases connected to authority transfers

### Editorial caution
Legitimate transfers can occur as part of a genuine company/operation sale with ownership/control properly recorded. Do not imply every authority transfer is fraudulent.

## 7. Broker liability / negligent hiring / small-carrier market effect

### Why it matters
Montgomery can change carrier-vetting, insurance, broker/provider behavior, and potentially the structure of usable trucking capacity.

### Rule
Do not repeat the Supreme Court background weekly. Include only when there is a fresh legal consequence, insurer response, vetting change, market practice, new litigation development, or measurable capacity effect.

### Fresh Aug. 27, 2026 signal to evaluate for next edition
FreightWaves interviews with transportation attorneys Brian Nelson and Greg Feary report that broker carrier-selection policies are becoming more formalized and that small, unrated, or conditional carriers may face greater difficulty remaining approved across broker networks. The article also cites SONAR data showing carriers with 1–6 power units declining from a 2023 peak near 222,000 to roughly 197,000 by mid-2026, while carriers with 15+ trucks increased slightly.

Attorney commentary also suggests some small carriers may increasingly lease onto or operate under larger carrier authorities as insurance, vetting, and legal pressures rise.

### Editorial framing for clients
- Treat the attorney comments as industry analysis, not an official regulatory finding.
- Do not claim Montgomery alone caused the decline in small carriers.
- The useful market angle is that independent small-carrier capacity may become harder to access directly as vetting standards tighten, even if the trucks/drivers remain in the market under larger carrier networks.
- Potential client impact: less independent spot flexibility, more selective carrier approval, and greater concentration of usable capacity among larger approved networks.

Source to revisit before publication:
- FreightWaves, Aug. 27, 2026 — “This May Not Be the Right Time to Jump Into the Small Carrier Market.” Two Top Transportation Attorneys on Whether the Small Carrier Has a Future.

## Weekly compliance selection test

A topic earns a place in the client-facing report only if at least one is true:
- new rule or proposal
- new deadline
- active enforcement date
- new measurable enforcement result
- new market/provider behavior
- new capacity consequence
- new fraud/safety risk with credible evidence

If none apply, omit it.
