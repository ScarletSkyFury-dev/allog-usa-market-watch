# Market Watchlist

Track these recurring market themes, but include them in the weekly report only when there is a fresh, material development or when the metric itself is a standard recurring field.

## Diesel / energy

Permanent weekly metric:
- EIA U.S. on-highway diesel by PADD + California
- national WoW / YoY
- lowest / highest region

Watch for:
- material crude-oil direction changes
- distillate inventory pressure when it changes the market outlook
- geopolitical/supply events likely to affect diesel

Client framing: direction and transportation-market pressure, not commodity-market technical detail.

---

## Transportation Capacity Pulse — LMI

Permanent monthly metric carried into weekly reports:
- Transportation Capacity
- Transportation Prices

Watch for:
- crossing above/below 50
- sharp MoM movement
- major YoY change
- unusually extreme readings

Interpretation:
- above 50 = expansion
- below 50 = contraction

Use alongside diesel and current port/terminal conditions. Never describe as drayage-specific.

---

## Small-carrier capacity structure / consolidation

Track fresh evidence that usable trucking capacity is shifting away from very small independent carriers toward larger carrier networks or authorities.

Current candidate for next edition (Aug. 27, 2026): FreightWaves interviews with transportation attorneys Brian Nelson and Greg Feary describe post-Montgomery carrier-selection and insurance pressure as particularly difficult for one-truck and small-fleet operators. The article cites SONAR data showing U.S. carriers with 1–6 power units declining from a 2023 peak near 222,000 to roughly 197,000 by mid-2026, while carriers with 15+ trucks ticked up slightly.

Watch for:
- continued decline in 1–6 truck authorities
- growth in larger-carrier counts or leased-on owner-operator models
- documented broker vetting-policy tightening
- reduced willingness to use conditional or unrated carriers
- insurance-cost or minimum-limit changes affecting small fleets
- evidence that independent small carriers are leasing onto larger authorities

Client framing: do not declare that small carriers are disappearing or that Montgomery caused the entire decline. Present this as a structural market signal: capacity may still exist, but a larger share may become accessible through bigger approved carrier networks rather than independent spot authorities. Potential effects include less spot flexibility, more selective carrier approval, and continued importance of carrier vetting.

Source to revisit before publication:
- FreightWaves, Aug. 27, 2026 — “This May Not Be the Right Time to Jump Into the Small Carrier Market.” Two Top Transportation Attorneys on Whether the Small Carrier Has a Future.

## Warehousing / transload

Permanent monthly metrics:
- Warehousing Capacity
- Warehousing Utilization
- Warehousing Prices

Watch for:
- capacity contraction
- utilization spikes
- price acceleration/deceleration
- regional port-adjacent warehouse availability when credible current data is available

Client impact: receiving flexibility, space reservation timing, short-term storage/transload availability, pricing direction.

---

## Flatbed / open-deck

Track weekly when available:
- rate movement
- YoY rate comparison
- load volume / capacity signal
- consecutive tightening/correction streaks when meaningful

Do not infer normalization from a short-term rate decline if YoY levels remain materially elevated.

---

## Ports / terminals / drayage

Watch only for current, specific developments:
- congestion / improving fluidity
- terminal consolidation or assignment changes
- appointments / gate restrictions
- empty-return restrictions
- material rail-connected dwell/backlog
- new road/terminal access
- labor action
- weather disruption

Do not maintain generic “ports to watch” filler.

---

## Rail / intermodal

Client-relevant developments:
- new or expanded service/frequency
- suspended service
- ramp opening/closure/restriction
- material dwell/backlog
- route change
- labor/weather disruption

Do not report rail fuel surcharges or internal rail cost mechanics.

Translate into cargo availability, transit time, pickup, delivery, or routing options.
