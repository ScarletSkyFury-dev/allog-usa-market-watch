# Metrics and Methods

## 1. DOE/EIA diesel

### Required weekly fields

- U.S. National Average
- WoW change
- YoY change
- PADD 1 / East Coast
- PADD 2 / Midwest
- PADD 3 / Gulf Coast
- PADD 4 / Rocky Mountain
- PADD 5 / West Coast
- California
- lowest-priced region
- highest-priced point
- EIA reference week
- release date
- next release date

### Map geography

Florida remains in **PADD 1 / East Coast**.

California is shown separately as the high-price inset even though it is within PADD 5.

### Interpretation

The weekly narrative should state:
- direction of national diesel
- whether all/most regions moved together
- the most notable regional move
- lowest and highest regions
- whether energy-market direction suggests increasing or easing future pressure

Avoid excessive wholesale-product detail. The client needs the market direction, not a commodities-trading report.

### Final-image gate

Do not produce the final weekly diesel map until the new EIA weekly diesel data is published. If the release is pending, label the report/map as pending and retain the prior values only as the latest official reference.

## 2. Logistics Managers’ Index (LMI)

LMI is a **monthly diffusion index**, not a weekly index.

### Interpretation rule

- **Above 50 = expansion**
- **Below 50 = contraction**

This rule applies to each LMI sub-index, but the business interpretation depends on the metric. For example:
- Transportation Capacity below 50 = available transportation capacity is contracting.
- Transportation Prices above 50 = transportation prices are expanding.
- Warehousing Capacity below 50 = warehouse availability is contracting.

### Permanent recurring metrics

#### Transportation
- Transportation Capacity
- Transportation Prices
- Transportation Utilization when useful

#### Warehousing
- Warehousing Capacity
- Warehousing Utilization
- Warehousing Prices

### Transportation Capacity Pulse

Transportation Capacity is a permanent macro capacity signal in the weekly report.

Use it to complement diesel and regional operating conditions when discussing:
- carrier flexibility
- spot availability
- broad trucking/drayage pressure
- quote stability

Important: **Transportation Capacity is not a drayage-specific index.** Never describe it as one.

Because LMI is monthly, carry forward the latest official reading until the next release. Do not imply a weekly change.

### Client-facing presentation rule

When Transportation Capacity and Transportation Prices are shown in the email, present them as separate bullets for fast visual interpretation, similar to the warehousing indicators.

Preferred format:
- **Transportation Capacity: [latest]** — [prior month] last month; [contraction/expansion] and [tightening/easing] direction.
- **Transportation Prices: [latest]** — [prior month] last month; [contraction/expansion] and [accelerating/slowing] direction.

When useful, add the same-month prior-year figure after the monthly comparison.

Do not merely state the latest number. Give the reader a benchmark and direction.

For the current July 2026 reference:
- Transportation Capacity: **28.4**, down from **30.8 in June** — deeper contraction.
- Transportation Prices: **86.9**, down from **92.4 in June** — still very strong expansion, but at a slower rate.

When useful, compare:
- latest month vs prior month
- latest month vs same month last year
- Transportation Capacity vs Transportation Prices

## 3. Flatbed / Open-Deck

Preferred recurring signals:
- spot rate movement in $/mile or percentage
- number of consecutive weeks of increase/decrease when meaningful
- YoY rate comparison
- load-volume change
- load-to-truck / rejection / capacity signal when available

Interpretation categories:
- tightening
- elevated but stabilizing
- seasonal correction
- structural normalization

Do not infer “normalization” merely because rates decline for one or several weeks if YoY rates remain materially elevated.

Client-relevant exposure includes project cargo, construction, energy, infrastructure, data centers, industrial equipment, oversized/overweight, permits, and specialized equipment.

## 4. Port / terminal / drayage signals

Useful metrics or advisories include:
- import dwell
- rail dwell
- vessel wait
- gate restrictions
- appointment constraints
- terminal consolidation
- terminal assignment changes
- empty-return restrictions
- road/access changes
- weather or labor disruption

### Dwell-method rule

Do not combine or confuse:
- rail dwell = time cargo waits at terminal before rail movement
- rail transit = movement time after departure

If a source combines metrics, say so.

A raw dwell number is not enough. Where possible provide:
- comparable normal/baseline range
- prior-week direction
- cause
- likely effect on cargo availability or final delivery

---

## 5. Rail / intermodal

Rail is included only when there is a client-relevant service development:
- new/expanded route or frequency
- service suspension
- ramp closure/opening/restriction
- significant backlog/dwell
- service interruption
- route change
- labor/weather disruption

Do not include rail fuel surcharges or internal rail pricing mechanics in client-facing reports.

Translate the development into:
- cargo availability
- transit time
- pickup timing
- final delivery timing
- routing alternatives

---

## 6. Warehousing / transload

Preferred signals:
- LMI Warehousing Capacity
- Warehousing Utilization
- Warehousing Prices
- credible regional vacancy/occupancy or provider-availability evidence when available

Client impact language:
- less/more receiving flexibility
- need for earlier space reservation
- firmer/softer short-term storage pricing
- tighter/easier transload windows

Do not turn this section into an internal operations checklist.

---

## 7. Compliance / carrier capacity

Compliance stories are included when enforcement or regulation can affect usable capacity or execution.

Useful categories:
- ELD removals and replacement deadlines
- English Language Proficiency enforcement/rulemaking
- non-domiciled CDL changes
- CDL-school / Training Provider Registry enforcement
- high-risk carrier investigations / shutdowns
- MC-number trafficking / carrier identity risk
- FMCSA modernization proposals
- broker-liability consequences when fresh

Always distinguish proposals from final/enforceable rules.

Client-facing question: **Could this reduce the number of drivers/carriers legally able to execute freight, increase vetting complexity, or create a service interruption risk?**
