# Source Hierarchy and Verification Rules

## Principle

Use the most authoritative source available for each claim. Secondary sources can discover or contextualize a story, but official/primary sources should confirm rules, deadlines, closures, service changes, and government data whenever possible.

## Tier 1 — Primary / preferred

### Fuel and energy
- U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA/DOE)
  - Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update
  - weekly on-highway diesel prices
  - PADD regional data
  - release schedule
  - short-term energy outlook / current energy indicators when useful

### Regulatory / compliance
- FMCSA
- U.S. Department of Transportation
- CVSA
- Federal Register
- FMCSA ELD Registry
- FMCSA Training Provider Registry
- official state DOT / DMV / enforcement agencies when the development is state-specific

### Ports / terminals
Prefer direct advisories and dashboards from the affected authority/terminal, including where relevant:
- Port of Los Angeles
- Port of Long Beach
- Port Authority of New York and New Jersey / individual terminal advisories
- Georgia Ports Authority
- South Carolina Ports Authority
- Port Houston
- Port of Virginia
- Oakland / Northwest Seaport Alliance / terminal-specific sources

### Rail / intermodal
Prefer direct service advisories from:
- BNSF
- Union Pacific
- Norfolk Southern
- CSX
- CN
- CPKC
- other operating railroads relevant to the reported corridor

### Broad rail traffic
- Association of American Railroads (AAR)

### Warehousing / transportation capacity
- Logistics Managers’ Index (LMI) official publication

## Tier 2 — Independent industry data / analysis

Useful for context, rates, market interpretation, or discovering developments:
- FTR / Truckstop Spot Market Insights
- DAT Freight & Analytics / DAT Trendlines
- FreightWaves / SONAR research
- Overdrive
- Transport Topics
- CCJ / FleetOwner and comparable specialist trucking media
- credible port, rail, and logistics trade publications

Where a Tier 2 article reports an official service change or rule, follow the source link and verify with Tier 1 when possible.

## Tier 3 — Supplemental / corroboration only

- Logistics-provider market updates
- brokerage market notes
- social/community signals from Reddit, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, X, etc.

These can reveal real-world sentiment or emerging operational pain, but should not be the sole authority for a client-facing factual claim.

## Competitor-source rule

Competitor research may be used internally to corroborate a trend, but avoid naming logistics competitors repeatedly in client-facing material when direct or independent sources exist.

## Freshness rules

For the weekly report:
- Prefer information published or operationally effective within the last 7–14 days.
- Older background is acceptable only when required to explain a current development.
- Past holiday schedules are stale once the event is over unless current residual backlog is documented.
- Regulatory topics recur only when there is a fresh rule, proposal, deadline, enforcement statistic, lawsuit effect, provider behavior change, or capacity impact.
- If the latest available index is monthly (e.g. LMI), explicitly state the reference month and carry the same value until the next release.

## Verification checklist

Before publishing a claim, confirm:
1. Is the source current enough for the statement?
2. Is it primary when a primary source should exist?
3. Does the source actually say what the draft claims?
4. Is the metric definition clear (e.g. dwell vs transit, LMI diffusion index)?
5. Is the comparison apples-to-apples?
6. Can we explain why it matters to the client?
7. Is the item still operationally relevant today?

## Social/community research

Community signals can be useful for:
- identifying accessorial pain points
- gauging owner-operator sentiment
- spotting emerging fraud patterns
- identifying early signs of regional capacity stress

Use them as directional intelligence, not as official evidence. Corroborate with industry data or primary sources when possible.
