# Editorial Standard

## Audience

The weekly report is written in Portuguese for two overlapping audiences:

- Allog internal colleagues who need a concise U.S. market pulse.
- Clients who hire Allog to manage U.S. inland delivery and related execution.

The report must therefore be operationally meaningful without reading like an internal dispatch checklist.

## Core writing model

Every meaningful paragraph should follow this logic:

**Indicator / event → context or benchmark → cause (if known) → client-facing effect.**

Examples:

- Bad: “Rail dwell is 6 days.”
- Better: “Rail dwell reached 6 days versus a lower normal operating range, which can delay cargo availability at destination and extend the final delivery timeline.”

- Bad: “Warehousing Capacity = 46.3.”
- Better: “Warehousing Capacity fell below the neutral 50 level, indicating contraction in available space; combined with rising utilization and prices, this suggests less flexibility for short-notice receiving and temporary storage.”

## Tone

- Professional, calm, factual.
- Client-friendly, not alarmist.
- Concise enough for email reading.
- Technical terms may remain in English where natural in U.S. logistics: drayage, flatbed, open-deck, rail ramp, appointment, empty return, transload, receiving, carrier vetting, etc.
- Explain unfamiliar events or indices briefly. Do not assume clients know names such as “Operation Safe Driver Week” or what an LMI score means.

## What not to do

### Do not shift Allog’s operational burden to the client

Avoid language such as:
- “Client should watch chassis/pre-pull/storage.”
- “Be careful with detention/demurrage.”
- “Validate empty return before dispatch.”

Those are Allog execution responsibilities.

Instead use:
- “Terminal conditions can reduce flexibility and require closer coordination.”
- “Allog is monitoring appointments, equipment availability, and terminal fluidity to preserve execution predictability.”

### Do not overuse “impacto operacional” blocks

The report should naturally explain why a development matters. Avoid repetitive subheadings followed by internal recommendations.

### Do not repeat background every week

Examples:
- ILA/USMX contract duration.
- Supreme Court broker-liability decision.
- ELP enforcement background.
- non-domiciled CDL background.

Bring them back only when a fresh deadline, new enforcement statistic, litigation consequence, provider behavior, insurance change, or market impact occurs.

### Do not use generic caution language

Avoid:
- “Ports require attention.”
- “Appointments and chassis should be monitored.”
- “Rail dwell remains relevant.”

If there is no specific current issue, omit or shorten the section.

## Recommended section behavior

### 1. Visão geral da semana

2–4 short paragraphs. Highlight only the most material changes of the week. Do not repeat the full report.

### 2. Diesel e mercado de energia

Lead with DOE/EIA diesel. Mention oil/energy only at the level needed to explain direction or volatility. Avoid unnecessary trading-market detail.

### 3. Flatbed / Open-Deck

Use actual weekly/annual data and explain whether conditions are tightening, correcting, or stabilizing.

### 4. Warehousing / Transload / Transportation Capacity

Use LMI and/or other credible market data. Always explain the 50-point diffusion-index threshold when LMI is cited.

### 5. Portos / Rail / Drayage

Only fresh, material, geographically specific developments. Rail coverage focuses on service availability and timing, not pricing mechanics.

### 6. Regulatório e Carrier Compliance

Fresh rules, deadlines, enforcement results, or proposals with operational consequences. Clearly label proposals as proposals.

### 7. Conclusão

Two short paragraphs maximum when possible. Summarize what matters now; do not restate every statistic.

## Competitor-source policy

Research from large logistics competitors may be useful for internal corroboration, but client-facing copy should preferentially cite official agencies, ports, terminals, railroads, index publishers, and independent market publications.

Avoid repeatedly naming competitors such as C.H. Robinson or Kuehne+Nagel in the final client-facing report when equivalent direct sources exist.
